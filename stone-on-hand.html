Take one useful photo

  1. Lay your hand and a standard credit or debit card flat on the same surface.
  2. Keep the camera directly above them to limit perspective distortion.
  3. Choose the photo, mark the card's 85.60 mm long edge, then place the stone.

The result is an approximate footprint, not a rendering of a finished setting or an appraisal.

The card sets the scale

The long edge of an ISO ID-1 payment card is 85.60 mm. Two clicks translate photo pixels into approximate millimeters at the card's plane.

Camera angle still matters

The card and finger must be flat on the same surface, with the camera directly above them. Perspective, lens distortion, and finger curvature can change the apparent scale.

The footprint is literal

Facet uses typical face-up dimensions for the chosen shape and carat. It does not simulate prongs, band width, setting height, sparkle, color, or exact stone proportions.

Stone on Hand is an open-source education tool made by Stienhardt & Stones, a New York City jeweler that sources certified Lab Grown Diamonds and hand-sets and finishes rings in NYC. Always compare the preview with the exact measurements on the stone's grading report.
